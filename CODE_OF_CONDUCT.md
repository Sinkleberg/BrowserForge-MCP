# Code of Conduct

This project follows the spirit of the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/) (version 2.1, CC BY 4.0), summarised here.

## Our pledge

We want participation in this community to be a harassment-free experience for everyone, whatever their age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behaviour

* Be kind and respectful. Assume good intent, and say so when you disagree.
* Give and accept constructive feedback gracefully.
* Take responsibility for mistakes, apologise, and learn from them.
* Focus on what is best for the community, not only for you.

## Unacceptable behaviour

* Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
* Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, personal or political attacks.
* Harassment, public or private.
* Publishing someone's private information without permission.
* Other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Responsible use

BrowserForge drives a real browser. Contributions that exist mainly to evade a site's protections, create accounts at scale, send spam, scrape private data or deceive people are not welcome here, and plugins or skills built for that will not be listed.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, issues and other contributions that do not follow this Code of Conduct, and may ban contributors for behaviour they judge harmful. To report a problem, use GitHub's private "Report content" tool or open a private security advisory on the repository so only the maintainers see it. Reports are handled promptly and confidentially.

Consequences follow the Contributor Covenant's enforcement guidelines: a correction, a warning, a temporary ban, or a permanent ban, depending on the severity and pattern of the behaviour.
